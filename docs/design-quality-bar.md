# Echo Design Quality Bar

Echo should feel human-made, not prompt-made. Before a feature is accepted, check it against this bar.

## Visual Identity

- Warm, quiet, and typographic.
- iPhone-first; desktop preview may frame the phone app, but should not become the main design language.
- Use a limited palette with ivory, warm charcoal, muted bronze, and very small gold accents.
- Avoid one-note color themes. Bronze is an accent, not a bath.
- No stock-looking hero graphics, no floating orb decoration, no generic AI gradients.

## Interaction Quality

- Creation should feel like entering a private room.
- Sealing should feel meaningful and final.
- Archive should feel like opening a preserved artifact.
- Hidden/locked content should never look like a normal list item.
- Every bottom-nav destination needs a reason to exist.

## Content Quality

- Copy should be short, clear, and emotionally restrained.
- Avoid therapy language unless a feature truly supports that responsibility.
- Avoid productivity language such as streaks, tasks, progress, metrics, and dashboards.
- Do not over-explain visible controls.

## Accessibility And Polish

- Every icon-only control needs an accessible name.
- Every input needs a label.
- Text must not overflow on a 390px-wide viewport.
- Interactive targets should be comfortable for touch.
- Use motion sparingly and respect reduced-motion preferences as the app matures.

## Red Flags

- A judge could say, "This looks like a template."
- The home screen becomes a dashboard.
- Locked Echoes become casually browsable.
- The app adds AI just because competitors mention AI.
- The UI uses impressive effects before the core reveal moment feels special.
